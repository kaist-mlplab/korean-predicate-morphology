Lexically conditioned realization ambiguity in Korean predicate morphology

Why a sequence of Korean morphemes and POS labels does not always determine the surface form: homonymous predicates in different conjugation classes

arXiv 2026

Wonjun Oh, KyungTae Lim, Jungyeul Park*

Korea Advanced Institute of Science & Technology (KAIST), South Korea

*Corresponding author

Paper (arXiv) PDF

Abstract

This paper examines Korean surface realization as distinct from morphological analysis. It asks whether a sequence of canonical morphemes and grammatical category labels uniquely determines the corresponding surface form. The answer is negative for a restricted but theoretically revealing class of Korean predicates. In these cases, formally identical or near-identical stem-ending configurations yield different outputs depending on lexical identity and realization class membership. We analyze this phenomenon as homonymy with inflectional divergence, focusing on regular versus digeut irregular pairs, regular versus bieup irregular pairs, and reu irregular versus reo irregular pairs. These cases show that stem shape and ending alone do not always determine surface realization. Instead, lexical meaning, subcategorization, and semantic role structure help identify the intended predicate; the predicate determines the realization class; and the realization class determines the surface form. Korean realization thus reveals a limit of bare morphological representation.

Analysis

The paper treats realization as the inverse of morphological analysis: given canonical morphemes with category labels, can the grammar recover a unique eojeol (the Korean spacing unit)? It distinguishes three relations between analysis and realization:

  1. Nearly transparent: {먹+었+다} → 먹었다 meogeotda ‘ate’.
  2. Non-transparent but locally predictable: {나서+었+다} → 나섰다 naseotda ‘stepped forward’, once the lexical item and morphophonological conditions are known.
  3. Underdetermined: {걷+었+다} yields 걸었다 georeotda ‘walked’ or 걷었다 geodeotda ‘cleared away’, depending on the intended lexeme.

The third case is called homonymy with inflectional divergence: predicates with the same or nearly the same stem shape belong to different realization (conjugation) classes. Using subcategorization frames from the Sejong dictionary, the paper argues for an indirect chain of explanation:

argument frame / semantic role pattern → lexical identity → realization class → surface form

Semantic roles do not choose the surface form directly; they help identify the lexeme, which carries its conjugation class. Derived predicates such as 치걷다 chigeotda, 되묻다 doemutda, and 파묻다 pamutda inherit the behavior of their simplex base.

Main Findings

  • Three ambiguity classes. Realization ambiguity among simplex predicates falls into regular vs. ㄷ digeut irregular (걷다, 닫다, 묻다), regular vs. ㅂ bieup irregular (곱다, 굽다), and 르 reu vs. 러 reo irregular (누르다, 이르다); the inventory in Table 2 is exhaustive for the Standard Korean Language Dictionary.
  • Stem shape marks only the site of alternation. The final consonant and ending show where an alternation could occur, but only the lexeme's realization class decides whether it does, so bare morphological representation is not a reversible encoding of surface form.
  • Attested in corpus data. In the KLUE dependency treebank (train + dev), the 걷다 family has 6 regular and 124 ㄷ-irregular tokens, 묻다 6 and 19, 닫다 6 and 13; 이르다 has 13 르-irregular and 20 러-irregular tokens; no token of 곱다 was found.
  • Practical consequence for reconstruction. Rebuilding surface eojeols from morpheme and POS input over KLUE, a refined rule-based system with explicit realization-class constraints reaches 92.56 target-eojeol F1 and 85.59 sentence-level accuracy, compared with 64.96 F1 for a previous rule-based baseline and 73.42 F1 (54.57 accuracy) for GPT-5.5.
